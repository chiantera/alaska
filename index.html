🔬 Understanding Arctic Sea Ice Measurements

🌡️ September Minimum

September marks the end of the Arctic summer melt season. After months of continuous sunlight, sea ice reaches its lowest annual extent. Scientists use this as the primary climate indicator because:

  • Shows the strongest climate change signal
  • Reveals cumulative summer melt effects
  • Determines wildlife habitat and shipping routes
  • Used by IPCC and NSIDC as the benchmark metric
❄️ March Maximum

March marks the end of the Arctic winter freeze season. After months of polar darkness, sea ice reaches its greatest annual extent. This metric shows:

  • Winter recovery capacity of the ice pack
  • Has declined less dramatically than September
  • Indicates multi-year ice formation potential
  • Important for seasonal sea ice dynamics

💡 Tip: Comparing both metrics reveals that while Arctic ice still recovers each winter, the summer minimums show accelerating decline—indicating the ice is becoming thinner and younger on average.
